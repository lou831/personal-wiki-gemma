## Summary

This syllabus outlines the 'Business of Energy Transition' course, which examines the massive capital reallocation occurring during the shift to a low-carbon energy system. The course uses a three-phase structure—landscape, toolkit, and frontier—to equip MBA students with the knowledge to identify and capitalize on investment opportunities in the clean energy sector.

## Key Points

- The course is structured in three phases: the landscape, the toolkit, and the frontier, covering the current state, sector-specific solutions, and future possibilities of the energy transition. ([[Business of Energy Transition Syllabus Fall 2026.pdf#page=2|p. 2]])
- Student group projects require a 12-slide presentation lasting 12 minutes, followed by an 8-minute Q&A, pitching a business or investment thesis. ([[Business of Energy Transition Syllabus Fall 2026.pdf#page=3|p. 3]])
- Week 8 frames the energy transition as, at its core, a financing problem: shifting from fuel-based operating expenses to capital investment and from centralized to decentralized energy means reinventing the financing system for energy. ([[Business of Energy Transition Syllabus Fall 2026.pdf#page=10|p. 10]])
- Students submit three written memos, one per third of the term, each at most two pages and written in the professional style of an investment thesis or business opportunity analysis. ([[Business of Energy Transition Syllabus Fall 2026.pdf#page=3|p. 3]])
- The toolkit weeks cover utility-scale power (Week 4), the grid edge with DERs and virtual power plants (Week 5), buildings (Week 6), finance (Week 8), and AI and data centers (Week 9). ([[Business of Energy Transition Syllabus Fall 2026.pdf#page=8|p. 8]])
- The final frontier phase addresses high-CAPEX technologies like Direct Air Capture and green hydrogen, requiring venture capital to bridge the 'valley of death'. ([[Business of Energy Transition Syllabus Fall 2026.pdf#page=14|p. 14]])

## Key Concepts

- [[Energy Transition]] — The course frames the energy transition as a reallocation of capital from fuel-based operating expenses to technology-based capital investment, a shift from a centralized to a decentralized system, and a move from carbon-based to carbon-free energy.
- [[Virtual Power Plants]] — A dynamic network of active participants utilizing Distributed Energy Resources (DERs) to aggregate and monetize flexible generation and demand at the grid edge.
- [[Corporate Procurement Strategies]] — The business logic behind major corporations driving renewable energy demand through mechanisms like Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) and Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs).
- [[Deep Tech Underwriting]] — The process where venture capital firms assess and provide capital for uncommercialized, high-CAPEX technologies like DAC and SMRs.
